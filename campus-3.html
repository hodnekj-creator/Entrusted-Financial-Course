4 Weeks, session 3 of 4

Debt and the fine print

Borrowing puts someone else in charge of part of your future paychecks. Understand exactly what you are signing, and keep it small.

Presenting? Page Down or the right arrow moves one section at a time.

Read these aloud. Tap a reference to open it in the NIV.

Scripture

  • Proverbs 22:7The borrower is slave to the lender.
  • Luke 14:28-30Sit down and count the cost before you build.
  • Romans 13:8Let no debt remain outstanding.
  • Proverbs 22:26-27Do not put up security for another's debts.

Everyone answers.

Opening question

Do you know your student loan balance and interest rate right now, without checking?

Teaching point 1 of 4

How a loan really works.

You borrow the principal and pay interest on what you still owe. A longer term lowers the payment and raises the total you pay. The lender is counting on you to look only at the monthly payment, so look at the total.

Teaching point 2 of 4

APR is what you pay. APY is what you earn.

APR is the yearly rate before compounding, which is how loans are advertised. APY includes compounding, which is how savings are advertised. A card at 24% APR compounding daily really costs about 27% a year.

Teaching point 3 of 4

Debt has a time and a place. Keep it minimal.

A reasonable student loan for a degree that raises your income, or a modest mortgage, is different from a car you cannot afford or a vacation on a card. Either way the borrower serves the lender, so borrow little and finish early.

Teaching point 4 of 4

Credit cards: paid in full every month, or not at all.

Hear the warning first: Dave Ramsey teaches cutting them up, and people spend more with a card than with cash, so the wise default is to skip them. If you use one, pay the full statement balance every month. The first month you cannot, stop using it.

Practical step

List what you owe as a debt snowball

Write down every loan with its balance, APR and minimum payment, and number them from the smallest balance to the largest. That is the debt snowball from Baby Step 2. Use the cheat sheet to check any new loan before you sign.

In the group

Discuss

  1. What surprised you when you added up what you owe?
  2. How would being debt-free at 27 change what you could say yes to?
  3. Do you spend differently with a card than with cash?
  4. What would you tell a freshman about borrowing?
