8 Weeks, session 7 of 8

Leader guide: Invest shrewdly

Think ahead like the shrewd manager, and aim it at what lasts. Small amounts, given time, become large ones.

Session pageHandout

Before you teach

  • Open the Tools page and practice the calculator.
  • Read Luke 16:1-13 in two translations.
  • Print handouts with both cheat sheets.

Caution

This is the riskiest passage in the course. The parable is not approval of dishonesty and not a proof-text for any investment. Teach verses 9-13 as Jesus' own explanation. Give no specific investment recommendations; this is teaching, not professional advice.

Teaching notes

Big idea

Think ahead like the shrewd manager, and aim it at what lasts. Small amounts, given time, become large ones.

Scripture

Luke 16:1-13
A manager about to lose his job uses his last days to secure his future, and the master commends his shrewdness.
Proverbs 13:11
Dishonest money dwindles, but whoever gathers little by little makes it grow.
Ecclesiastes 11:2
Invest in seven ventures, even eight; you do not know what disaster may come.
Proverbs 28:20
A faithful person will be richly blessed, but one eager to get rich will not go unpunished.

Opening question

If you had started saving $100 a month at 18, what do you think it would be worth today?

Teaching points

  1. The master praises foresight, not dishonesty.

    The manager saw what was coming and acted while he still could. Jesus says people of the world are often sharper about their future than God's people are about theirs. His application (verses 9-13): use worldly wealth for eternal ends, be faithful with little, and do not serve money.

  2. Little by little is how it grows.

    $200 a month from age 22 to 65 at an assumed 7% return grows to about $655,000 from $103,200 of contributions. Start at 32 and it is about $309,000. Ten years of waiting costs more than half.

    The 7% return is an assumption for illustration. Returns are not guaranteed.

  3. Take the match, then build to 15%.

    An employer match is an immediate return on your money, which is why it comes before debt payoff in the steps. After the reserve is full, build retirement investing to 15% of income.

  4. Diversify, and distrust anything fast.

    Ecclesiastes says to spread it across many ventures because you do not know what is coming. Proverbs 28:20 warns the person in a hurry to get rich. Broad, low-cost, boring and automatic beats clever.

Practical step: Check your match and run your numbers

Find out whether your employer matches and up to what percent. Use the compound growth calculator with your own monthly amount, then run it again starting ten years later. Learn the difference between traditional and Roth accounts from the cheat sheet.

Discussion questions

  1. What does the shrewd manager do that you are not doing?
  2. What are you investing in that will matter in 100 years?
  3. Why is get-rich-quick so attractive? Where have you seen it go wrong?
  4. What is stopping you from starting, or from increasing, this month?

This week's action

  • Confirm you are getting the full employer match.
  • Run your own numbers in the calculator and write down the cost of waiting.

Likely objections

Is investing just gambling?
Gambling is a wager on chance. Investing is owning a share of productive businesses over decades. Proverbs praises gathering little by little.
Should I invest before my debt is gone?
Take the match, because it is an immediate return. Everything beyond the match waits until consumer debt is gone and the reserve is full.
What about returns? I heard 12%.
Use a conservative number such as 7%. If the market does better, that is a bonus, not a plan.
