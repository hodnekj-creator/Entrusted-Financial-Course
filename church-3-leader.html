8 Weeks, session 3 of 8

Leader guide: Know your flocks

A faithful manager knows exactly what he has and decides ahead of time where it goes. That is a budget.

Session pageHandout

Before you teach

  • Bring your own budget, or last month's actuals, as a real example.
  • Print two budget sheets per person.
  • Decide which tracking tools you will mention.

Caution

Keep the tone practical. Shame makes people stop looking at their numbers, which is the opposite of the goal.

Timed plan

45 minutes in all.

Teaching notes

Big idea

A faithful manager knows exactly what he has and decides ahead of time where it goes. That is a budget.

Scripture

Proverbs 27:23-24
Know the condition of your flocks and give careful attention to your herds, because riches do not last forever.
Luke 14:28-30
No one builds a tower without first sitting down to estimate the cost.
Proverbs 21:5
The plans of the diligent lead to profit; haste leads to poverty.

Opening question

Without looking it up, how much did you spend on food last month? How confident are you?

Teaching points

  1. A budget is knowing your flocks.

    A shepherd who never counted would lose sheep without noticing. Most people lose money the same way. Counting is not a lack of faith; Proverbs calls it wisdom.

  2. Decide before the month starts.

    Jesus assumes a sensible builder counts the cost first. A budget written in advance is a plan. A list of what you already spent is only a report.

  3. Every dollar gets a job.

    Income minus every line should equal zero. Giving and saving are the first lines, because whatever is left to the end usually is not there.

  4. The budget serves you.

    The first three months will be wrong, and that is normal. Adjust the plan, not the honesty. Couples should write it together; a budget only one spouse knows about is not a shared plan.

Practical step: Write next month's budget

Start with income after tax. Write giving first, then saving, then needs, then wants, until the remainder is zero. Track actual spending for one month in whatever tool you will really use: an app, a spreadsheet or an envelope of cash.

Discussion questions

  1. What comes to mind when you hear the word budget: freedom or restriction? Why?
  2. Which category do you least want to look at?
  3. If you are married, who knows the numbers? Is that working?
  4. What would you do with money you found by tracking?

This week's action

  • Write a budget for next month before it starts.
  • Track every dollar for 30 days.

Likely objections

I do not make enough to budget.
The less there is, the more each dollar needs a job. Tight margins are where counting pays most.
Budgets never work for me.
Usually the first one was abandoned after one bad month. Expect three months of adjusting.
Is planning this much a lack of trust in God?
Proverbs praises the planner. Trust shows in how you hold the plan, not in refusing to make one.
