Debt and the fine print

Borrowing puts someone else in charge of part of your future paychecks. Understand exactly what you are signing, and keep it small.

Teaching notes

How a loan really works.

APR is what you pay. APY is what you earn.

Debt has a time and a place. Keep it minimal.

Credit cards: paid in full every month, or not at all.

Discuss

  2. How would being debt-free at 27 change what you could say yes to?
  3. Do you spend differently with a card than with cash?
  4. What would you tell a freshman about borrowing?

List what you owe and choose your order

Write down every loan with its balance, APR and minimum payment. Number them smallest balance first (snowball) and highest rate first (avalanche), and pick one. Use the cheat sheet to check any new loan before you sign.

Loans and rates cheat sheet

TermWhat it means
PrincipalThe amount you borrowed and still owe.
InterestWhat the lender charges for the use of the money.
APRAnnual percentage rate: the yearly rate on a loan, before compounding. This is the number you pay.
APYAnnual percentage yield: the yearly rate including compounding. This is the number you earn on savings.
Why both existA card at 24% APR that compounds daily really costs about 27% a year. Lenders show the smaller number, banks advertise the bigger one.
TermHow long the loan lasts. A longer term means a lower payment and more total interest.
Minimum paymentThe least you can pay without a penalty. On a card it is set so the balance lasts for years.
Co-signingYou owe the whole debt if the other person stops paying. See Proverbs 22:26-27.

Debt payoff plan

Snowball: smallest balance first, for momentum. Avalanche: highest APR first, for the lowest total cost. Pay minimums on all, and put every extra dollar on the first one in your order.
