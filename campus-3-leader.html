Leader guide: Debt and the fine print

Borrowing puts someone else in charge of part of your future paychecks. Understand exactly what you are signing, and keep it small.

Session pageHandout

Before you teach

  • Work the 24% APR example so you can explain it plainly.
  • Print the cheat sheet and payoff plan.
  • Open the debt payoff calculator on the Tools page.

Caution

The credit card point is the one most likely to be heard as permission. Say the warning first and louder than the allowance. Do not teach that all debt is sin, and do not shame anyone for loans they already have.

Teaching notes

Big idea

Borrowing puts someone else in charge of part of your future paychecks. Understand exactly what you are signing, and keep it small.

Scripture

Proverbs 22:7
The borrower is slave to the lender.
Luke 14:28-30
Sit down and count the cost before you build.
Romans 13:8
Let no debt remain outstanding.
Proverbs 22:26-27
Do not put up security for another's debts.

Opening question

Do you know your student loan balance and interest rate right now, without checking?

Teaching points

  1. How a loan really works.

    You borrow the principal and pay interest on what you still owe. A longer term lowers the payment and raises the total you pay. The lender is counting on you to look only at the monthly payment, so look at the total.

  2. APR is what you pay. APY is what you earn.

    APR is the yearly rate before compounding, which is how loans are advertised. APY includes compounding, which is how savings are advertised. A card at 24% APR compounding daily really costs about 27% a year.

  3. Debt has a time and a place. Keep it minimal.

    A reasonable student loan for a degree that raises your income, or a modest mortgage, is different from a car you cannot afford or a vacation on a card. Either way the borrower serves the lender, so borrow little and finish early.

  4. Credit cards: paid in full every month, or not at all.

    Hear the warning first: people spend more with a card than with cash, so the wise default is to limit or skip them. If you use one, pay the full statement balance every month. The first month you cannot, stop using it.

Practical step: List what you owe and choose your order

Write down every loan with its balance, APR and minimum payment. Number them smallest balance first (snowball) and highest rate first (avalanche), and pick one. Use the cheat sheet to check any new loan before you sign.

Discussion questions

  1. What surprised you when you added up what you owe?
  2. How would being debt-free at 27 change what you could say yes to?
  3. Do you spend differently with a card than with cash?
  4. What would you tell a freshman about borrowing?

This week's action

  • Log in and write down every loan balance and rate.
  • Fill in the debt payoff plan and pick snowball or avalanche.

Likely objections

Don't I need a credit card to build credit?
A card paid in full every month does build credit. So do on-time rent and loan payments. Credit is a tool for borrowing, so do not go into debt to earn the right to more debt.
Should I pay loans off fast or invest?
Take any employer match first. After that, clear consumer debt before investing more.
Everyone has a car payment.
Everyone having one is why it feels normal, not why it is wise. Count the total cost, not the payment.
