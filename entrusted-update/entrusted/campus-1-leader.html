4 Weeks, session 1 of 4

Leader guide: Whose money is it?

God owns everything you will ever earn. Your job is to manage it faithfully, starting with the small amounts you have now.

Session pageHandout

Before you teach

  • Read Matthew 25:14-30 twice and note what surprises you.
  • Fill in the inventory yourself first.
  • Print one step card and one handout per person.
  • Warn people before they write that a negative inventory total is normal.

Caution

The parable is about faithfulness while the master is away. Do not teach it as a promise of wealth, and do not skip the third servant: his problem starts with his view of the master.

Teaching notes

Big idea

God owns everything you will ever earn. Your job is to manage it faithfully, starting with the small amounts you have now.

Scripture

Psalm 24:1
Everything on earth belongs to the Lord.
1 Chronicles 29:14
David admits that what he gives to God came from God first.
Matthew 25:14-30
The parable of the talents: a master entrusts his wealth to three servants and returns to settle accounts.
Colossians 3:23-24
Work with all your heart, as working for the Lord.

Opening question

If someone handed you $10,000 tomorrow with no strings attached, what would you do with it?

Teaching points

  1. God owns it. You manage it.

    Psalm 24:1 leaves nothing out. A manager makes real decisions and has real freedom, but he never forgets whose name is on the account. Picture house-sitting: you live there and make choices every day, and you still do not repaint the kitchen. The $10,000 always has strings attached.

  2. The manager's job is faithfulness, not size.

    The servant with two bags hears the same words as the servant with five. A graduate on a $45,000 salary and one on $90,000 are measured the same way: what did you do with what you were handed? The parable does not promise that faithful people get rich. It promises that the master notices.

  3. Work is the first way you manage.

    Colossians 3:23-24 says to work with your whole heart, as working for the Lord. Your skills, degree and health are part of what you were entrusted with, and Deuteronomy 8:17-18 says the ability to earn is itself a gift. The verse is about who you work for, not about working every waking hour.

  4. Giving shows who you think the owner is.

    The tithe is Old Testament law and a good practice to learn from. The New Testament instruction in 2 Corinthians 9:7 is to give what you have decided in your heart, cheerfully. Decide on purpose and pray over the number. If giving has never stretched you, pray over it more. The habit is easiest to start on a small first paycheck, before a lifestyle fills in around it.

Practical step: Take inventory

A manager cannot be faithful with what he has not counted. Fill in the inventory: what you hold, what you owe, what comes in. Then read the step card once from top to bottom. The rest of the course walks through it.

Discussion questions

  1. Where do you most naturally think "this is mine": your paycheck, your time, your stuff?
  2. The third servant acted out of fear. Where does fear drive your money decisions?
  3. What would change this week if you were managing your money for someone you had to report to?
  4. What is one thing you are already managing well?

This week's action

  • Fill in the "What I manage" inventory.
  • Decide what you will give from your next paycheck, and pray over the number before you settle it.

Likely objections

I earned it, so it is mine.
Go to Deuteronomy 8:17-18. Effort is real, and the ability behind it was given.
I am broke, so this does not apply yet.
Go to Luke 16:10. Habits form at small numbers, and someone with $500 is already a manager.
Does God want me to be rich?
The parable makes no such promise. Read Proverbs 30:8-9 together.
