8 Weeks, session 6 of 8

Get out of debt

The borrower serves the lender. Baby Step 2 is to pay off everything except the house, smallest balance first.

Presenting? Page Down or the right arrow moves one section at a time.

Read these aloud. Tap a reference to open it in the NIV.

Scripture

  • Proverbs 22:7The rich rule over the poor, and the borrower is slave to the lender.
  • Romans 13:8Let no debt remain outstanding except the continuing debt to love one another.
  • Proverbs 22:26-27Do not put up security for debts; if you cannot pay, your bed will be taken from under you.
  • Psalm 37:21The wicked borrow and do not repay, but the righteous give generously.

Everyone answers.

Opening question

What is the first thing you ever borrowed money for? Would you do it again?

Teaching point 1 of 4

Scripture warns about debt without forbidding it.

Proverbs 22:7 describes what debt does: it puts someone else in charge of part of your future income. Lending and borrowing appear throughout the Bible, but never as the wise default.

Teaching point 2 of 4

Baby Step 2: all of it, except the house.

Cards, car loans, student loans, medical bills, personal loans: all of it goes. Debt for something that lasts is different from debt for things already used up, but either way what you have borrowed you repay (Psalm 37:21), and the sooner it is gone the sooner your income is yours to direct.

Teaching point 3 of 4

Credit cards: paid in full, or not at all.

Dave Ramsey teaches cutting them up, and people do spend more when they pay with a card, so skipping cards is wise. If you use one, the rule is simple: the full statement balance is paid every month. The first month it is not, the card goes in a drawer.

Teaching point 4 of 4

The debt snowball.

List your debts from the smallest balance to the largest. Pay the minimum on all of them and attack the smallest with every spare dollar. When it is gone, roll its payment into the next one. Each payoff frees more money for the next, and the quick wins keep you going.

Practical step

Build your debt snowball

List every debt except the house with its balance, APR and minimum payment, smallest balance first. Pay minimums on everything and all extra on the smallest. When it is gone, roll its payment onto the next, and write the date each one is paid off.

In the group

Discuss

  1. How does owing money affect your freedom to give, change jobs, or say yes to God?
  2. Which would keep you going: quick wins or the lowest total cost?
  3. If you use a credit card, do you spend differently than you would with cash?
  4. Has anyone asked you to co-sign? What does Proverbs 22:26-27 say to that?

Before we meet again

This week

  • Fill in your debt snowball, smallest balance first.
  • Find one expense to cut and send that amount to the smallest debt this month.
