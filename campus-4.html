4 Weeks, session 4 of 4

Small things, long time

Little things now make a big difference later. Time is the one advantage you have that older people cannot buy.

Presenting? Page Down or the right arrow moves one section at a time.

Read these aloud from your own Bibles.

Scripture

  • Proverbs 13:11Whoever gathers money little by little makes it grow.
  • Luke 16:10Whoever can be trusted with very little can also be trusted with much.
  • Luke 16:1-13The shrewd manager acts today because he sees what is coming.
  • Proverbs 13:22A good person leaves an inheritance for their children's children.

Everyone answers.

Opening question

Would you rather have $1 million today, or a penny doubled every day for a month? Why?

Teaching point 1 of 4

Compound interest is growth on growth.

Your money earns a return, and then the return earns a return. For the first ten years it looks like nothing is happening. The last ten years do most of the work, which is why the early years matter most.

Teaching point 2 of 4

The cost of waiting.

$200 a month from age 22 to 65 at an assumed 7% grows to about $655,000. The same $200 from age 32 grows to about $309,000. Waiting ten years saves you $24,000 in contributions and costs you about $346,000.

The 7% return is an assumption for illustration. Returns are not guaranteed.

Teaching point 3 of 4

Take the match, and know your accounts.

If your employer matches contributions, take all of it from your first paycheck; it is an immediate return. A 401(k) comes through your job. A Roth IRA you open yourself, pay tax on now, and withdraw tax-free in retirement, which often suits a first-job income.

Teaching point 4 of 4

Shrewd for the right reasons.

The shrewd manager is praised for seeing ahead and acting, and Jesus points that foresight at eternity. Faithful with little comes before trusted with much. The goal is not a big number; it is a life that can give freely and leave something for the next generations.

Practical step

Run your own numbers

Open the compound growth calculator. Enter an amount you could really invest each month from your first job. Write down the value at 65, then change the start age by ten years and write that down too. Mark where you are on the step card.

In the group

Discuss

  1. What small habit now would your 60-year-old self thank you for?
  2. What makes it hard to care about something 40 years away?
  3. How could what you build serve other people, not only you?
  4. Which step are you on, and what is your next one?

Before we meet again

This week

  • On your first day at a job with a match, sign up for the full match.
  • Write your cost-of-waiting number on the handout and keep it.
