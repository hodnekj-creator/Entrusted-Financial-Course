4 Weeks, session 2 of 4

Keep living like a student

When the first real paycheck arrives, keep spending like you did in college. The gap is your one-time head start.

Presenting? Page Down or the right arrow moves one section at a time.

Read these aloud from your own Bibles.

Scripture

  • Proverbs 21:20The wise store up; fools consume all they have.
  • Proverbs 21:5The plans of the diligent lead to profit; haste leads to poverty.
  • 1 Timothy 6:6-8Godliness with contentment is great gain; with food and clothing we will be content.
  • Luke 12:15Life does not consist in an abundance of possessions.

Everyone answers.

Opening question

What is the first thing you plan to buy when you get a full-time salary?

Teaching point 1 of 4

You already know how to live on little.

Right now you live on a fraction of what you will earn. The day you start a salary, your income might triple. If your spending stays near where it is, the difference can fund giving, a buffer and debt payoff within a couple of years.

Teaching point 2 of 4

Lifestyle creep is the default.

Nobody decides to spend everything. The apartment gets nicer, the car gets newer, the subscriptions stack up, and each one feels earned. Proverbs 21:20 calls consuming all you have foolish, however large the income.

Teaching point 3 of 4

Contentment is learned early or expensively.

Paul says contentment is great gain. Learning it at 22 on a small budget is far cheaper than learning it at 40 with a mortgage and two car loans.

Teaching point 4 of 4

Write the budget before the paycheck.

Know your flocks (Proverbs 27:23). Give first, save second, then live on what remains. Your first target is the starter buffer: one month of essentials.

Practical step

Write your first-paycheck budget

Fill in what you spend now in each category. Then fill in the salary column, keeping every spending line as close to today as you can. Whatever is left goes to giving, the starter buffer and loans.

In the group

Discuss

  1. What part of student life would you be glad to keep for two more years? What part would be hard?
  2. Whose lifestyle are you tempted to match after graduation?
  3. What does enough look like for you at 25?
  4. What would you do with an extra $1,000 a month for two years?

Before we meet again

This week

  • Fill in the first-paycheck budget.
  • Add up one month of essentials: that is your starter buffer target.
