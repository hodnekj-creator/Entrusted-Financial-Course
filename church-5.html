8 Weeks, session 5 of 8

Build margin

Wise people keep something in store. Margin turns an emergency into an inconvenience and frees you to be generous.

Presenting? Page Down or the right arrow moves one section at a time.

Read these aloud. Tap a reference to open it in the NIV.

Scripture

  • Proverbs 21:20The wise store up choice food and oil; fools consume all they have.
  • Proverbs 6:6-8The ant stores provisions in summer and gathers at harvest.
  • Genesis 41:34-36Joseph stores grain in seven good years to carry Egypt through seven lean ones.
  • Matthew 6:25-34Do not worry about tomorrow; your Father knows what you need.

Everyone answers.

Opening question

What was your last unexpected expense, and how did you pay for it?

Teaching point 1 of 4

Emergencies are certain. Only the date is unknown.

Cars break, jobs end, people get sick. Joseph did not know which year the famine would start, only that it would. Saving for it is prudence, not pessimism.

Teaching point 2 of 4

Baby Step 1: $1,000, fast.

The starter emergency fund is small on purpose so you can finish it quickly, often in a month or two. It is the barrier that keeps the next repair or copay from going on a card while you attack your debt.

Teaching point 3 of 4

Baby Step 3: three to six months.

Once everything but the house is paid off, build the full emergency fund. Three months of expenses if income is steady and shared, six if it is irregular or you are the only earner.

Teaching point 4 of 4

Margin is not hoarding.

Jesus tells us not to worry, and the rich fool in Luke 12 is condemned for storing up only for himself. The reserve has a limit and a purpose. Past that limit, money has other jobs.

Practical step

Size your emergency fund

Work out how many months it will take you to save $1,000. Then add up one month of essentials and multiply by three and six: that is your Baby Step 3 target. Keep it in a separate savings account, and set an automatic transfer on payday.

In the group

Discuss

  1. Would a $1,500 surprise this month go on a card, come from savings, or come from family?
  2. Where is the line between wise saving and hoarding?
  3. How does having margin change your ability to give when a need appears?
  4. What could you cut or sell to reach $1,000 faster?

Before we meet again

This week

  • Open a separate savings account and schedule the first transfer toward $1,000.
  • Calculate your one-month number and your three-to-six-month target.
