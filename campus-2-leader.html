4 Weeks, session 2 of 4

Leader guide: Keep living like a student

When the first real paycheck arrives, keep spending like you did in college. The gap is your one-time head start.

Session pageHandout

Before you teach

  • Work out what you spent per month in college versus now, as an honest example.
  • Print both worksheets.
  • Look up typical rent in your area to make the numbers real.

Caution

This is wisdom, not a rule that enjoying anything is wrong. The point is the order: give, save, then spend, and do not let the first salary set a lifestyle you then have to feed.

45 minutes in all.

Teaching notes

Big idea

When the first real paycheck arrives, keep spending like you did in college. The gap is your one-time head start.

Scripture

Proverbs 21:20
The wise store up; fools consume all they have.
Proverbs 21:5
The plans of the diligent lead to profit; haste leads to poverty.
1 Timothy 6:6-8
Godliness with contentment is great gain; with food and clothing we will be content.
Luke 12:15
Life does not consist in an abundance of possessions.

Opening question

What is the first thing you plan to buy when you get a full-time salary?

Teaching points

  1. You already know how to live on little.

    Right now you live on a fraction of what you will earn. The day you start a salary, your income might triple. If your spending stays near where it is, the difference can fund giving, an emergency fund and debt payoff within a couple of years.

  2. Lifestyle creep is the default.

    Nobody decides to spend everything. The apartment gets nicer, the car gets newer, the subscriptions stack up, and each one feels earned. Proverbs 21:20 calls consuming all you have foolish, however large the income.

  3. Contentment is learned early or expensively.

    Paul says contentment is great gain. Learning it at 22 on a small budget is far cheaper than learning it at 40 with a mortgage and two car loans.

  4. Write the budget before the paycheck.

    Know your flocks (Proverbs 27:23). Give first, save second, then live on what remains. Your first target is Baby Step 1: $1,000 in a starter emergency fund.

Practical step: Write your first-paycheck budget

Fill in what you spend now in each category. Then fill in the salary column, keeping every spending line as close to today as you can. Whatever is left goes to giving, the $1,000 starter emergency fund and then your loans.

Discussion questions

  1. What part of student life would you be glad to keep for two more years? What part would be hard?
  2. Whose lifestyle are you tempted to match after graduation?
  3. What does enough look like for you at 25?
  4. What would you do with an extra $1,000 a month for two years?

This week's action

  • Fill in the first-paycheck budget.
  • Open a separate savings account for your $1,000 starter emergency fund.

Likely objections

I have waited four years to stop being broke.
Two more years of living simply buys decades of margin. And you choose what to loosen first; just choose it on purpose.
My salary will not be that high.
Then the gap is smaller and matters more. The habit is the same at any income.
Isn't saving this much a lack of faith?
Proverbs calls storing up wise. Faith shows in giving first and not trusting the savings.
