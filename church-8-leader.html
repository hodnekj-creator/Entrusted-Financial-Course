8 Weeks, session 8 of 8

Leader guide: Legacy and contentment

The account outlives you. Leave an inheritance, give generously while you live, and learn the word enough.

Session pageHandout

Before you teach

  • Check your own beneficiaries before you teach this.
  • Bring the step card for everyone to mark.
  • Print handouts.

Caution

A proverb describes how life generally goes; it is not a guarantee or a command to die rich. Read Proverbs 13:22 beside Luke 12 and Proverbs 30:8-9 so the session does not drift toward a prosperity message.

45 minutes in all.

Teaching notes

Big idea

The account outlives you. Leave an inheritance, give generously while you live, and learn the word enough.

Scripture

Proverbs 13:22
A good person leaves an inheritance for their children's children.
1 Timothy 6:6-10
Godliness with contentment is great gain; the love of money is a root of all kinds of evil.
1 Timothy 6:17-19
The rich are told not to hope in wealth but to be rich in good deeds, generous and willing to share.
Luke 12:13-21
The rich fool builds bigger barns for himself and is not rich toward God.

Opening question

What did you inherit from your parents or grandparents that was not money?

Teaching points

  1. Think in generations.

    Proverbs 13:22 looks two generations ahead. Most financial decisions look two weeks ahead. An inheritance includes money, and also faith, character and the know-how to handle what is passed on.

  2. An inheritance can bless or harm.

    Proverbs 20:21 warns that an inheritance gained too soon is not blessed at the end. Preparing the heir matters as much as preparing the money. Teach children to work, give and save while they are watching you do it.

  3. Contentment is the guardrail.

    Paul says he learned contentment (Philippians 4:11-13). Without a point called enough, step 6 becomes bigger barns. Decide your enough before you reach it.

  4. Give while you live.

    1 Timothy 6 tells those with wealth to be rich in good deeds now. A will handles what is left; generosity during your life is something you get to see and your family gets to learn from.

Practical step: Put your legacy in order

Work through the checklist: a current will, named beneficiaries on every account, someone who knows where things are, enough insurance for those who depend on you. Then mark which Baby Step you are on and name your next one.

Discussion questions

  1. What do you want your grandchildren to say about how you handled money?
  2. What would be enough for you? Have you ever written the number down?
  3. How do you hold together leaving an inheritance and not building bigger barns?
  4. Which step are you on now, and what is the next one?

This week's action

  • Complete the legacy checklist and fix one gap this month.
  • Mark your step on the step card and tell one person what your next step is.

Likely objections

I have nothing to leave.
Start with what is not money: faith, example, skills. Then a will and beneficiaries, which cost little.
Shouldn't I give it all away instead?
Scripture commends both inheritance and radical generosity. Pray over the balance; the rich fool's error was keeping it all for himself.
Do I need a will if I am young?
If anyone depends on you, or you own anything, yes. Beneficiary forms take ten minutes.
