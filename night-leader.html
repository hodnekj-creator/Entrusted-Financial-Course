One Night

Leader guide: Entrusted in one night

God owns it all, we manage it, and He will ask for an account. Here is the whole course in 90 minutes.

Session pageHandout

Before you teach

  • Read Matthew 25:14-30 and Luke 16:1-13 twice.
  • Print a step card, handout and website card for each person.
  • Decide who will read each passage aloud.
  • Mark your own step first so you can go first in discussion.

Caution

Do not try to teach all seven steps; the conversation is the point. This version leaves out budgeting mechanics, loan detail, account types and wills. Say so at the end and point people to the site or a longer track.

Teaching notes

Big idea

God owns it all, we manage it, and He will ask for an account. Here is the whole course in 90 minutes.

Scripture

Psalm 24:1
Everything on earth belongs to the Lord.
Matthew 25:14-30
The parable of the talents.
Colossians 3:23-24
Work with all your heart, as working for the Lord.
2 Corinthians 9:7
Give what you have decided in your heart, cheerfully.
Proverbs 22:7
The borrower is slave to the lender.
Luke 16:1-13
The shrewd manager.
Proverbs 13:22
A good person leaves an inheritance for their children's children.

Opening question

What is the best and the worst money decision you have made?

Teaching points

  1. Owner and manager.

    God owns all of it, and a manager is measured on faithfulness, not on the size of what he was handed. The two-bag servant hears the same words as the five-bag servant. The third servant's failure began with a wrong view of the master.

  2. Work and giving.

    Work is done for the Lord, and the ability to earn is a gift. The tithe is Old Testament law and a good practice; the New Testament instruction is to give what you have decided in your heart. If giving has never stretched you, pray over it more.

  3. The steps.

    Read the step card once. Then spend the time on the two steps most people are stuck between: the starter buffer of one month of essentials, and getting out of consumer debt. Have each person privately mark the step they are on.

  4. The long view.

    The master praises the shrewd manager's foresight, not his dishonesty, and Jesus applies it to using money for what lasts. $200 a month from age 22 reaches about $655,000 by 65 at an assumed 7% return, and about $309,000 if started at 32. Proverbs 13:22 looks two generations ahead; it is wisdom, not a guarantee.

    The 7% return is an assumption for illustration. Returns are not guaranteed.

Practical step: Find your step

Mark the step you are on. Write down the one thing that is stopping the next one. Take the inventory home and fill it in this week.

Discussion questions

  1. Which step are you on, and what is stopping the next one?
  2. Where do you most naturally think "this is mine"?
  3. For couples: do you agree with each other on which step you are on?
  4. What is one thing you will do before we meet next?

This week's action

  • Fill in the "What I manage" inventory at home.
  • Do the one thing you wrote down, and tell someone in the group when it is done.

Likely objections

That was a lot. Where do I start?
With the inventory and one step. Nothing else this week.
We disagree as a couple.
Normal. Fill in the inventory together before discussing any decision.
Can we go deeper?
Yes: the four-week and eight-week tracks cover each piece with its own handout.
