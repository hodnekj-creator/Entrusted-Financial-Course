The Stewardship Steps

Seven steps, taken in order. Giving and a written budget apply at every one of them.

Print the step card

At every step

Give first and keep a written budget.

Proverbs 3:9-10; Proverbs 27:23

Giving first is how a manager acknowledges the owner. A budget is how a manager knows the condition of what he has been handed.

  1. 1

    Step 1: Starter buffer

    Save one month of essential expenses.

    Proverbs 21:20

    The wise keep something in store. A month of essentials turns most emergencies into inconveniences, so a flat tire does not become new debt.

    How you know you are done

    You could cover rent, food, utilities, insurance and transport for one month with no paycheck.

  2. 2

    Step 2: Take the match

    Contribute enough to get any employer retirement match.

    Proverbs 13:11

    A match is money gathered little by little that someone else adds to. Passing it up is turning down part of your pay.

    How you know you are done

    You are contributing at least the percentage your employer matches. No match offered? Skip to step 3.

  3. 3

    Step 3: Clear consumer debt

    Pay off consumer debt and keep all debt minimal.

    Proverbs 22:7; Romans 13:8

    The borrower serves the lender. Debt has a time and a place, but it should stay small and have an end date. A credit card is only a tool if it is paid in full every month; people spend more on cards, so limiting or skipping them is wise.

    How you know you are done

    No car loans, personal loans, or card balances carried past the statement date.

  4. 4

    Step 4: Full reserve

    Build 3 to 6 months of expenses.

    Proverbs 6:6-8; Genesis 41

    The ant stores in summer and Joseph stored in the good years. A full reserve covers a job loss, not just a repair.

    How you know you are done

    Three months of expenses saved if your income is steady, six if it is irregular or you are the only earner.

  5. 5

    Step 5: Invest 15%

    Invest 15% of income for retirement.

    Ecclesiastes 11:2; Proverbs 21:5

    Diligent plans lead to profit, and spreading investments guards against what you cannot foresee. Time does most of the work, so starting early matters more than starting big.

    How you know you are done

    15% of gross income is going into retirement accounts every month, automatically.

  6. 6

    Step 6: Fund major goals

    Save for a home, children's education and other large goals.

    Proverbs 24:27; Luke 14:28

    Get your fields ready, then build your house. Count the cost before you start so the goal does not undo the steps before it.

    How you know you are done

    Each large goal has a number, a date and its own account.

  7. 7

    Step 7: Finish and leave it better

    Pay off the home, build wealth, leave an inheritance, give generously.

    Proverbs 13:22; 1 Timothy 6:17-19

    A good person leaves an inheritance for their children's children, and those who have much are told to be rich in good deeds and ready to share. Wealth at this step is a larger thing to manage, not a finish line.

    How you know you are done

    No debt of any kind, a current will, and giving that grows faster than your lifestyle.
