8 Weeks, session 6 of 8

Leader guide: Get out of debt

The borrower serves the lender. Debt has a time and a place, but it should stay small and have an end date.

Session pageHandout

Before you teach

  • Work one example with real-looking numbers in both orders.
  • Print handouts.
  • Have the debt payoff calculator ready on the Tools page.

Caution

Do not teach that all debt is sin; the text does not say it. Do not shame people in debt. Do be plain that a card balance carried month to month is the line.

Teaching notes

Big idea

The borrower serves the lender. Debt has a time and a place, but it should stay small and have an end date.

Scripture

Proverbs 22:7
The rich rule over the poor, and the borrower is slave to the lender.
Romans 13:8
Let no debt remain outstanding except the continuing debt to love one another.
Proverbs 22:26-27
Do not put up security for debts; if you cannot pay, your bed will be taken from under you.
Psalm 37:21
The wicked borrow and do not repay, but the righteous give generously.

Opening question

What is the first thing you ever borrowed money for? Would you do it again?

Teaching points

  1. Scripture warns about debt without forbidding it.

    Proverbs 22:7 describes what debt does: it puts someone else in charge of part of your future income. Lending and borrowing appear throughout the Bible, but never as the wise default.

  2. Keep it minimal, and repay what you owe.

    Debt for something that lasts, with a payment you can clearly afford and a date it ends, is very different from debt for things already used up. What you have borrowed, you repay (Psalm 37:21).

  3. Credit cards: paid in full, or not at all.

    People spend more when they pay with a card, so limiting or skipping cards is wise. If you use one, the rule is simple: the full statement balance is paid every month. The first month it is not, the card goes in a drawer.

  4. Pick a payoff order and attack.

    Snowball pays the smallest balance first and builds momentum. Avalanche pays the highest rate first and costs the least. Both work if you stop adding new debt and put every spare dollar on one target.

Practical step: Build your payoff plan

List every debt with its balance, APR and minimum payment. Number them in snowball order and in avalanche order, then choose one. Pay minimums on everything and all extra on the first. When it is gone, roll its payment onto the next.

Discussion questions

  1. How does owing money affect your freedom to give, change jobs, or say yes to God?
  2. Which would keep you going: quick wins or the lowest total cost?
  3. If you use a credit card, do you spend differently than you would with cash?
  4. Has anyone asked you to co-sign? What does Proverbs 22:26-27 say to that?

This week's action

  • Fill in the debt payoff plan and pick your order.
  • Find one expense to cut and send that amount to the first debt this month.

Likely objections

Is a mortgage wrong?
No. It is the clearest case of debt with a time and place. Keep it small relative to income and aim to finish it.
Which is better, snowball or avalanche?
Avalanche by math, snowball for many people by follow-through. The best one is the one you finish.
I get rewards on my card.
Rewards are real only if the balance is paid in full and you are not spending more to get them. Most people spend more.
